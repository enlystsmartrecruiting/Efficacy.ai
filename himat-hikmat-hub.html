02 · Theoretical Framework · Sen · TPACK · Lipsky

Three bodies
of evidence.

Strand 01 · Institutional Economics
Capability-Based Development

Drawing on Sen's capability approach and Nussbaum's central human capabilities, the framework argues that educational outcomes should be assessed not by test scores alone but by the capabilities they enable. The 24.3% deficiency is a capability deficit, not merely an academic gap.

Sen, A.K. (1999) · Development as Freedom · Nussbaum, M. (2011) · Creating Capabilities
Strand 02 · Technology in Education
TPACK — Pedagogical Content Knowledge

The TPACK framework provides the lens for analysing teacher readiness. The research operationalises TPACK competency against the specific constraints of Pakistan's government school environment — procurement restrictions, data sensitivity, and infrastructure limitations that commercial TPACK literature does not address.

Mishra & Koehler (2006) · TPACK Framework · Adapted for developing-economy constraints
Strand 03 · Governance & Accountability
Street-Level Bureaucracy — Lipsky

Lipsky's theory explains why top-down educational policies systematically fail to reach the classroom in the intended form. Teachers are not passive implementers — they are active discretion-exercisers operating under resource constraints. The governance lens applies this to Punjab's education inspection and reporting systems.

Lipsky, M. (1980) · Street-Level Bureaucracy · Applied to Punjab Education Department

The name is a
positioning statement.

In Urdu, Himat means courage — the willingness to look at an uncomfortable truth and name it precisely. Hikmat means wisdom — the capacity to move from that truth toward workable, durable solutions. The framework requires both simultaneously.

The research was not designed to produce comfortable conclusions. It was designed to produce useful ones — ones that can survive political and institutional contact without losing their integrity.

Research Methodology

Mixed-methods design: quantitative regression on primary student outcome dataset combined with qualitative structured teacher interviews and institutional document analysis. Government-reported aggregate data used only as baseline comparison.

04 · Disability Education Data · Punjab

The leaky pipeline.
By the numbers.

Field statistics from Punjab's 36 government special education schools — primary data, not government-reported aggregates. Every number was counted in a classroom.

Welfare Coverage
100%

Welfare stipend reach among enrolled students. The financial access problem has been solved. Enrolment grew 18% post Himmat Card launch. Students are physically present.

Himat Card Programme · Punjab Special Education Dept · 2024
Teacher Proficiency
21.1%

Teachers meeting the proficiency threshold for disability-specific pedagogy. Four in five teachers cannot deliver the specialised instruction their students require — despite holding BEd and MEd credentials.

Primary field survey · N=90 teachers · Punjab special schools · 2024
Classroom Tool Availability
13.3%

Schools with adequate mathematics teaching tools (Taylor Frames, Braille Embossers, Nemeth Code materials). The remaining 86.7% teach mathematics verbally — without the tools the curriculum assumes exist.

School audit · 36 government special education schools · Punjab · 2024
Nemeth Code Proficiency
73.3%

Teachers lacking Nemeth Braille Code skills — the essential system for mathematical notation in Braille. Without Nemeth proficiency, mathematics instruction for visually impaired students is structurally impossible beyond primary level.

Teacher competency assessment · Himat-Hikmat field research · 2024
Secondary Attrition
24.3%

Students who drop out or fail between Grade 8 and Grade 10 matriculation. The stipend kept them enrolled through primary — but the absence of Hikmat means they cannot succeed at secondary level and remain lifetime welfare dependents.

Controlled regression · N=37,214 · p<0.001 · University of Punjab
Geographic Disparity
38.3%

Gap in pass rates between central divisions (Lahore: ~100%) and peripheral divisions (Bahawalpur: 58.3%). Structural inequality is not random — it follows the resource gradient from centre to periphery with brutal consistency.

Divisional analysis · 9 Punjab divisions · Himat-Hikmat research · 2024
