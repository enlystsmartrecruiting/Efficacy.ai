The Framework

Four lenses.
One argument.

"Structural inequality — not teacher effort — is the primary driver of learning deficiency. Resource allocation at district level explains 68% of the variance in primary school outcomes."

The framework is structured around four analytical lenses — each addressing a distinct failure layer in Pakistan's public education system. All four converge on a single policy conclusion: the problem is architectural, not motivational. It requires systems reform, not sympathy.

Himat-Hikmat is Pakistan's most rigorous indigenous theoretical model for mathematics education among visually impaired students. Every statistic was gathered through primary field visits, structured teacher interviews, and student assessment sampling — not government-reported aggregate data.

I
Lens 01
Structural Inequality

How resource allocation models at federal, provincial, and district level create systemic disadvantage that individual teacher effort cannot overcome — regardless of motivation or training.

II
Lens 02
Technology Readiness Gap

The delta between the curriculum's stated digital learning goals and the actual technological infrastructure and teacher competency available at the point of instruction in government schools.

III
Lens 03
Governance Accountability

How performance measurement and accountability chains in provincial education bureaucracy create perverse incentives — where reported metrics diverge systematically from classroom reality.

IV
Lens 04
Policy Translation

The gap between research evidence and legislative action in Pakistan's education sector — and the institutional design required to close that gap within a single provincial policy cycle.

55%
Teacher AI Readiness Gap
GITTC Lahore · Government AI Training Centre · 2024–2025

Government school teachers with no exposure to AI-assisted lesson planning tools. Of the 45% with any exposure, fewer than 12% report consistent classroom use.

<12%
Consistent AI Use in Classrooms
GITTC Lahore cohort survey · 2024–2025

Of the teachers with any AI tool exposure, fewer than 12% use them consistently in classroom delivery. Infrastructure, time, and confidence are the three cited barriers.

Policy Implication

From thesis
to legislation.

The research proposes mandatory AI literacy standards for all new government school teacher appointments from 2027 — embedded into PPSC testing criteria and BISE curriculum review cycles.

This is not aspirational. It is a dated, documented, peer-reviewed proposal submitted to the Punjab Education Commission within a defined submission window.

Vision
Teaching Teachers of Disabled Students

Taking the research
into classrooms.

Structured capacity-building workshops across Punjab — sessions built from the evidence base, not from good intentions.

01
Workshop Design

Built from the evidence base — not generic training content. Sessions are structured around specific competency gaps identified through the field research. Targeted capacity building tied to measurable outcomes, not attendance certificates.

02
Who Attends

Teachers from government and private special education schools. Priority cohorts include those with high visually impaired enrolment and low pass rates — the schools where structural failure is most visible and most costly.

03
What We Measure

Pre- and post-workshop competency assessments. Every workshop produces a dataset that feeds back into improving both the research and the training. No workshop is run without measurement. No measurement sits idle.

Foundation Governance

Ring-fenced.
By design.

The Foundation operates under strict separation from commercial revenues and commercial influence. These are not aspirational commitments. They are architectural constraints.

The Foundation does not exist to make the business look good. It exists because the problem is real, the data is ours, and the obligation cannot be outsourced.

Wasim Hassan — Founder · Enlyst Foundation
01
Revenue Ring-Fence

No commercial revenue from enlyst, enlysum, enlybiz, or enlysoft is used to fund Foundation research. The Foundation is funded independently through grants, partnerships, and direct contributions.

02
Research Independence

No commercial client has editorial influence over research conclusions, publication timing, or policy positions. Findings are published as documented — not sanitised for business comfort.

03
Data Stewardship

All school visit data, student outcome data, and teacher interview data is anonymised, stored securely, and never used as marketing collateral without researcher consent.

04
Researcher Primacy

Dr. Imran Sabir and the doctoral research team hold final authority over all published findings. The Foundation is the enabler, not the author.
