Primary Research Output

The Himat-Hikmat Framework

Himat-Hikmat — Urdu for Courage and Wisdom — is a comprehensive indigenous Pakistani theoretical model for mathematics education among visually impaired (VI) students in Punjab. It is the product of six years of field research, mixed-methods doctoral study, and direct engagement with students, teachers, and government administrators.

The framework exposes a systemic "Leaky Pipeline" where government financial stipends are disbursed to VI students without any corresponding investment in instructional capacity. Students receive money, but not knowledge. Teachers receive directives, but not training. The pipeline leaks — and Pakistan's most vulnerable learners fall through.

The Himat-Hikmat Framework proposes a three-tier reform: teacher competency standards, curriculum adaptation protocols, and independent instructional audits — all supported by an evidence base of 37,100 words across six research chapters.

Read the Full Framework Document

The Leaky Pipeline — Key Findings

  • • 24.3% student attrition from Grade 8 to matriculation among VI learners
  • • 34-percentage-point achievement gap across Punjab divisions
  • • Teacher variables explain 61% of achievement variance
  • • Only 21% of teachers meet competency thresholds (vs. 84% international benchmark)
  • • Stipends disbursed but no structured instructional capacity in place

What the Framework Proposes

  • 1. Mandatory teacher competency standards for VI education across Punjab
  • 2. Adapted mathematics curriculum aligned to Braille and tactile learning methods
  • 3. Independent instructional audits separate from school administration
  • 4. Provincial legislative proposal to codify these standards into law

Active Programme

Teaching Teachers of Disabled Students

The Foundation's most direct field activity — taking the Himat-Hikmat research into classrooms through structured capacity-building workshops for teachers working with visually impaired students across Punjab.

Workshop Design

Each workshop is built from the Himat-Hikmat Framework's evidence base. Sessions are structured around the specific competency gaps identified in the field research — not generic training, but targeted capacity building tied to measurable outcomes.

Who Attends

Teachers from government and government-assisted special education schools across Punjab. Priority cohorts include mathematics teachers at schools with high VI enrolment and low matric pass rates — the most critical intervention points in the pipeline.

What We Measure

Pre- and post-workshop competency assessments. Follow-up classroom observation visits. Student outcome tracking at six and twelve months. Every workshop produces a dataset that feeds back into the framework's evidence base — improving both the research and the training.

Personal Engagement — Foundation Activities

Doctoral Field Research

Six-chapter sequential explanatory mixed-methods study — combining quantitative data collection across Punjab schools with qualitative interviews of teachers, students, and administrators.

School Visits Across Punjab Divisions

Direct classroom observation, student interviews, and administrator engagement across multiple Punjab divisions — documenting the 34-percentage-point achievement gap and its structural causes.

Teacher Capacity Workshops

In-person and blended-format workshops for teachers of VI students — structured to address the specific 79% competency gap documented in the research. Includes mathematics pedagogy for tactile learning, Braille curriculum alignment, and IEP (Individualised Education Plan) development.

Data Visualisation & Policy Reports

Translating complex field data into visual policy documents accessible to government administrators — bridging the gap between academic research and legislative decision-making.

Government Liaison & Policy Advocacy

Presenting research findings to Punjab Education Department officials and SECP stakeholders — building the case for legislative reform through evidence rather than advocacy alone.

Publication Preparation

Preparing the Himat-Hikmat Framework for peer-reviewed academic journal submission and international conference presentation — establishing the research as a citable, reproducible contribution to inclusive education literature.

Foundation Roadmap

Where the Enlyst Foundation is going.

A five-year roadmap — from doctoral research to national policy impact.

2026

Thesis Completion & Peer-Review Submission

Complete the doctoral thesis (six chapters, 37,100+ words). Submit the Himat-Hikmat Framework to at least two peer-reviewed journals in inclusive education and educational psychology. Present at one international conference on disability-inclusive education.

2027

Provincial Legislative Proposal

Convert the framework's policy recommendations into a formal legislative proposal for the Punjab Assembly — mandating teacher competency standards, adapted curriculum, and independent audit mechanisms for VI education. Engage Punjab Education Department for pilot implementation across two divisions.

2028

Scale Teacher Training Across All Punjab Divisions

Expand the "Teaching Teachers" programme to all nine Punjab divisions — reaching every government special education school with structured capacity-building workshops, pre/post competency assessment, and ongoing mentoring support for VI mathematics teachers.

2029

National Replication — All Four Provinces

Replicate the Himat-Hikmat Framework across Sindh, KPK, and Balochistan — adapting for regional language and curriculum variations. Establish the Enlyst Foundation as Pakistan's leading independent research body for disability-inclusive education policy.

2030

International Peer Recognition & South Asia Expansion

Establish international partnerships with disability education bodies in the UK, UAE, and South Asia. Develop a replicable framework export model for deployment in comparable low-resource, high-VI-population contexts. Position Pakistan as a knowledge exporter in inclusive education — not just an aid recipient.
