Himat-Hikmat · Finding 01

The infrastructure governance gap

Doctoral field work across Punjab’s government school catchment (tens of thousands of children in scope) keeps returning to the same pattern: devices, labs, and platforms arrive without the institutional routines that make them usable — scheduling, maintenance, teacher time, and accountability.

The policy argument is unflinching: treat governance as infrastructure. Courage (Himat) names the gap; wisdom (Hikmat) designs the operating cadence. Enlyst Foundation rings this research off from commercial revenue.

Himat-Hikmat · Finding 02

Teacher AI readiness

Field signals point to a substantial readiness gap — often summarised around a ~55% “AI unready” band — combining tool access, pedagogical confidence, and institutional permission. Training that ignores civil-service constraints and liability frameworks fails before it starts.

Enlyst AI Academy’s GovTech track exists because literacy programmes must match approved tools and public-sector realities — not Silicon Valley demos. enlyst · Practice note

Documented hiring in Pakistan

Referral culture is real. Signalling noise is real. EOBI and onboarding compliance are real. Enlyst treats documentation — briefs, anonymised dossiers, scorecards, decline notes, 30/90-day follow-up — as a hard constraint, not a brand flourish.

We design processes to reduce bias. We do not claim magical certainty. If a role cannot be filled honestly, we say so. That is the credibility the live site claimed — this page exists so the nav keeps that promise.
